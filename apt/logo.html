Apt · by Provenance — brand mark

One glyph. Proven, and rising.

The Apt mark is a single stroke that reads two ways at once — a checkmark (every claim proven at the Provenance Gate) that overshoots into an upward arrow (a visitor apt to convert; CTA and ROI rising). Proof and lift, in one move — exactly what makes a page apt.

What it means

✓

The check = proven

Every claim on an Apt page is cited or killed at the Provenance Gate. The mark opens as a checkmark.

↗

The arrow = apt to convert

The check doesn’t stop — it overshoots upward. Relevance turns into lift: higher CTA, lower CAC, rising ROI.

◎

One continuous stroke = fit

Proof and performance are the same motion, not a trade-off. That’s the whole thesis of “apt.”

Clear space & usage

✓ Clear space

  • Keep padding of at least the mark’s arrowhead height on all sides.
  • Minimum mark size 20 px; wordmark stays legible above 90 px wide.
  • Gradient runs bottom-left → top-right, always.

✓ Do

  • Use the gradient mark on solid dark or light backgrounds.
  • Use the mono (currentColor) mark when one ink is required.
  • Pair the mark with apt. — lowercase, gradient period.
  • Let the mark stand alone once the brand is known.

✕ Don’t

  • Rotate, flip, or reverse the arrow’s upward direction.
  • Recolor the gradient or add a drop shadow to the stroke.
  • Add barbs, round the arrowhead off, or change stroke weight.
  • Set the wordmark in a different or condensed typeface.
